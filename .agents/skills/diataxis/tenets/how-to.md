# Tenets: how-to guides

The how-to specification, extracted from diataxis.fr `/how-to-guides/` and `/map/` (fetched 2026-09-17).
Companion specs: [core.md](core.md), [tutorial.md](tutorial.md), [reference.md](reference.md), [explanation.md](explanation.md).

## What a how-to guide is

- "How-to guides are **directions** that guide the reader through a problem or
  towards a result. How-to guides are **goal-oriented**." "A how-to guide
  helps the user get something done, correctly and safely; it guides the
  user's _action_." It "is concerned with _work_". Answers "How do I…?"; form:
  a series of steps; analogy: a recipe in a cookery book.
- "**How-to guides must be written from the perspective of the user, not of
  the machinery.**" "How-to guides are about goals, projects and problems, not
  about tools. Tools appear in how-to guides as incidental bit-players, the
  means to the user's end." Anti-pattern: guides "defined by operations that
  can be performed with a tool or system... not addressed to any need the user
  has"; "shocking how often how-to guides in software documentation are
  written at this level" (e.g. "turn the tap clockwise").
- Characteristics: "focused on tasks or problems / assume the user knows what
  they want to achieve / action and only action / no digression, explanation,
  teaching". "A how-to guide serves the work of the already-competent user."
- "**How-to guides are wholly distinct from tutorials**... Conflating them is
  at the root of many difficulties that afflict documentation." They are "not
  merely procedural guides": "Real-world problems do not always offer
  themselves up to linear solutions. The sequences of action... sometimes need
  to fork and overlap, and they have multiple entry and exit-points."

## Principles

- Maintain focus on the goal.
- Address real-world complexity: "adaptable to real-world use-cases".
- Omit the unnecessary: "practical usability is more helpful than
  completeness... It should start and end in some reasonable, meaningful
  place".
- Provide a set of instructions: an _executable solution_. "Actions" includes
  "thinking and judgement".
- Describe a logical sequence.
- Seek flow: "At its best, how-to documentation gives the user flow... the
  documentation equivalent of a helper who has the tool you were about to
  reach for, ready to place it in your hand".
- Pay attention to naming: "Choose titles that say exactly what a how-to guide
  shows": good "How to integrate…"; "Integrating…" is weaker; a bare noun
  title is the weakest choice.
- A how-to guide list "helps frame the picture of what your product can
  actually _do_"; "Well-written how-to guides that address the right questions
  are likely to be the most-read sections of your documentation".
- Recipe analogy: a recipe "excludes both teaching and discussion, and focuses
  only on **how**"; even a professional chef follows a recipe; "Even following
  a recipe **requires at least basic competence**... a recipe is not a
  substitute for a cooking lesson."

## Language

- "This guide shows you how to…"
- Conditional imperatives: "If you want x, do y. To achieve w, do z."
- "Refer to the x reference guide...". "Don't pollute your practical how-to
  guide with every possible thing."
