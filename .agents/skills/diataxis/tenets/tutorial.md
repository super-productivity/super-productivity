# Tenets: tutorials

The tutorial specification, extracted from diataxis.fr `/tutorials/` and `/map/` (fetched 2026-09-17).
Companion specs: [core.md](core.md), [how-to.md](how-to.md), [reference.md](reference.md), [explanation.md](explanation.md).

## What a tutorial is

- "A tutorial is an **experience** that takes place under the guidance of a
  tutor. A tutorial is always **learning-oriented**." "A tutorial is a
  _practical activity_, in which the student learns by doing something
  meaningful, towards some achievable goal." (tutorials)
- "Its purpose is not to help the user get something done, but to help them
  learn." It "is a lesson." Answers "Can you teach me to…?"; form: a lesson;
  analogy: teaching a child how to cook. (tutorials, map)
- Teacher/pupil contract: "nearly all the responsibility falls upon the
  teacher... the only responsibility of the pupil in this contract is to be
  attentive and to follow the teacher's directions... There is no
  responsibility on the pupil to learn, understand or remember." The exercise
  must be meaningful, successful, logical, usefully complete. (tutorials)
- "A good lesson gives the learner confidence, by showing them that they can be
  successful in a certain skill or with a certain product." (tutorials)

## Principles

- The first rule of teaching: **don't try to teach**. Instead, "allow learning
  to take place... Give your learner things to _do_".
- Anti-pedagogical temptations: "abstraction, generalisation / explanation /
  choices / information". (tutorials)
- Show the learner where they're going: set expectations up front. "In this
  tutorial we will create and deploy...". The banned form is "you will learn…",
  "which is presumptuous and a very poor pattern". (tutorials)
- Deliver visible results early and often: "Every step the learner follows
  should produce a comprehensible result, however small".
- Maintain a narrative of the expected ("You will notice that…"); point out
  what the learner should notice; target "the feeling of doing".
- Encourage and permit repetition: "Repetition is not the best teacher -
  sometimes it's the _only_ teacher".
- "Ruthlessly minimise explanation": "A tutorial is not the place for
  explanation... Explanation is only pertinent at the moment the _user_ wants
  it. It is not for the documentation author to decide". Link to explanation
  "so that it's available, but doesn't get in the way".
- Focus on the concrete: "All learning moves in one direction: from the
  concrete and particular, towards the general and abstract".
- Ignore options and alternatives: "Your guidance needs to remain focused on
  what's required to reach the conclusion".
- Aspire to perfect reliability: "Your tutorial ought to be so well
  constructed that things _can't_ go wrong, that your tutorial works for every
  user, every time".
- "You are required to be present, but condemned to be absent."

## Language

First-person plural: "The first-person plural affirms the relationship between tutor and learner: you are not alone; we are in this together."
Patterns:

- "In this tutorial, we will…"
- "First, do x. Now, do y."
- "The output shows…"
- "Notice that…"
- End by describing (mildly admiring) what the learner accomplished.
