6. Data Processing during Synchronization

A) Transport

  • Your content data is transmitted via TLS/SSL transport encryption.
  • Data that older app versions uploaded without end-to-end encryption, before the Service required it, may still be stored unencrypted in our database until it is deleted, at the latest with your account (Section 8 (1)). Access to it by the Provider is technically possible but occurs exclusively if required for maintenance, diagnosis, or defense against technical disturbances.

B) End-to-End Encryption (E2EE)

The Service accepts synchronized data only end-to-end encrypted:

  • Your data is encrypted locally on your device before transmission.
  • The server stores only encrypted data blocks ("Blobs").
  • We have no access to your keys and cannot restore, decrypt, or view the data.
  • Loss of the key results in permanent data loss.

C) AI Assistant Connections (optional)

If you let AI assistants add tasks (Settings → Sync → SuperSync → AI assistants) and connect one, for example Claude, ChatGPT or a script using a manual token, the assistant's provider can send new tasks to the Service on your behalf:

  • What an assistant sends. A task's title, notes and, if given, its date, time and repetition. We handle this text only in memory: we encrypt it so that only your own devices can open it, store it only encrypted and never write it to logs. To avoid adding the same task twice when an assistant repeats a request, we keep a scrambled one-way code of it (not the text itself) in memory. It is used for 10 minutes and deleted within a day after that, or when the server restarts.
  • Tasks your own devices send this way (for example from the Android share sheet) are encrypted on the device; we never see their content.
  • Waiting tasks. With each encrypted task we store when it arrived, which connection sent it and which of your devices picked it up. It is deleted once a device has imported it, otherwise within a day after {{ CAPTURE_RETENTION_DAYS }} days.
  • Connections. For each connected assistant we store:
    • whether it connected by signing in or with a manual token, and its name (for a manual token, the name you enter);
    • the identifier the assistant's app uses to connect;
    • when the connection was created, renewed, last used (updated at most once an hour), when it expires and when it was removed;
    • scrambled one-way forms (hashes) of its access credentials, never the credentials themselves.
    A connection lasts until 90 days without renewal (a manual token: up to one year).
  • Connecting. Connection codes and pending connections (the assistant's identifier, the address to return it to and technical values that secure the step) expire after at most 10 minutes. They are deleted once used, otherwise within a day after they expire.
  • Your capture key. We store its public part, which lets assistants encrypt tasks for you but cannot open them. The private part that opens them is part of your synchronized settings, which the Service accepts only end-to-end encrypted, so we cannot open your tasks.
  • Abuse limits. We count connection attempts per IP address (an IPv6 network counts as one) and, per connection, its requests and new tasks, and whether it was used in the last 24 hours. These counts exist only in the server's memory, never in the database or logs. Each covers at most 24 hours and is deleted within a day after that, or when the server restarts.
  • What the assistant's provider receives. A connected assistant can only add tasks; it cannot read your data through the Service. We send its provider the access credentials for the connection and, per request, technical protocol data and a short confirmation or error message. These contain no task content; an error message may repeat the name of a field the assistant sent that we do not accept. We send them because you asked us to connect the assistant (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). For users in the EU/EEA, the provider is usually its EU company (for example Anthropic Ireland or OpenAI Ireland), so this is a disclosure within the EU. For users outside the EU/EEA, the provider is usually its US company (for example Anthropic PBC), with which you have your own contract. No adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses between us and the provider cover this; we send these data only because you connected that assistant.
  • The provider is a recipient of these data (Art. 4(9) GDPR). It receives what you tell the assistant, including the task text, before it reaches us. You use the assistant under your own agreement with its provider. Depending on that agreement, the provider processes your data as a separate controller under its own privacy policy, or, for a business plan, on behalf of your organisation; we have no influence on it. For that processing, contact the provider or your organisation; for what we send it, contact us.
  • Removing connections. Under Settings → Sync → SuperSync → AI assistants → Connected assistants, "Revoke" stops an assistant at once. Tasks it already sent stay until imported or expired, unless you choose to delete them too. Signing out on all devices also removes every connection and keeps waiting tasks. Switching the feature off there removes every connection and deletes all waiting tasks. Records of removed or expired connections are deleted within a day. We keep only your public capture key, marked as switched off, so that an outdated device cannot switch the feature back on without you; it is deleted with your account (Section 8 (1)), as is all other data of this feature.
  • Counting. We may count, by a manual database query, connections per kind of assistant (manual tokens without their names) and how many were used in the last 30 days. Only totals that cannot identify anyone are kept; they contain no task content.

Legal bases:

  • Performance of the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), because you request the feature by switching it on and connecting an assistant: receiving and storing waiting tasks, connection records, connection codes and your public capture key, and sending the provider the access credentials and messages described above.
  • Our legitimate interest in protecting the Service and its users against automated misuse and overload (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR): the abuse limits.
  • Our legitimate interest in knowing which kinds of assistants are used, so that we can size and maintain this feature (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR): the counting.

Right to object: you can object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to processing based on our legitimate interests (Art. 21 GDPR), by email to {{ PRIVACY_CONTACT_EMAIL }}. We then leave your connections out of future counting. The abuse limits we continue only where compelling legitimate grounds override your interests, as they protect all users of the Service.
